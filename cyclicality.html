📖 How to read this page — plain English

First, the honest part: this chart cannot tell you which direction today goes. We tested that four different ways — same-weekday history, every averaging method, look-alike days — and direction is a coin flip every time. What IS knowable: how much the market moves and what hours it usually moves in. Everything below is about using those two things.

① The prediction line + the cloud around it (agreement band). The line is the last 4 same-weekdays mashed together. The cloud shows how much those 4 weeks disagreed — its top is the best of them, its bottom the worst, at every minute. Fat cloud covering both sides of zero = the weeks all did different things → the line is noise today, skip it. Skinny cloud all on one side = the weeks actually rhymed — the only kind of day the line deserves a glance. (Rare.)
Why Median is the default method: we tested all four against reality (990 days). Median's predicted day-size missed by ~33 pts on average — the closest; Sum missed by ~70, because Sum stacks the 4 weeks on top of each other and draws the same line ~4× too tall (that's why it never looks like the smooth actual line). Direction is a coin flip on every method, so pick the one with honest scale.

② Ghost lines. Pick a single weekday and you'll see the 4 prior weeks drawn faintly. One glance shows whether the prediction is honest seasonality or just one crazy week dragging the average.

③ "Expected movement today" (the card above). Take the last 20 days, measure each day's total travel (high to low), average them. That's the most accurate movement forecast we found — it beat every fancy method. Two rules make it useful:

  • The market finishes about HALF as far as it travels. Card says 72 → expect to end ~36 pts from the open. A straddle pays on the finish, not the travel.
  • Compare the 9:32 straddle price to half the card. Straddle quote below half-the-card = movement is on sale. Above = you're overpaying.

④ The colored strip at the bottom (movement intensity). It's the market's daily nap schedule: one cell per 5 minutes — red = this hour is usually rowdy on this weekday, blue = usually asleep. The marks on top are when YOU show up: Straddle 9:32 · GXBF 9:36 · Tail 9:45 · Diagonal 12:30 · shaded box = your M8BF window. Put your finger on your entry mark and read the colors to the RIGHT of it — that's the weather your trade must live through. A fly is a tent: you want blue after you pitch it (red right after your M8BF window = pitching a tent before the storm hours — size accordingly). A straddle is a sled: it needs a hill — you want red in the morning. All-blue morning with red only at 15:30 = your straddle sits flat for hours; the move, if any, comes late.

⑤ The dashed cyan cone (live days only). During market hours it takes today's actual price and shows how the typical same-weekday finishes from here, with its spread. Holding a fly into the close? Cone inside your tent = normal script. Cone edge far outside your wings = the typical finish breaks your trade — a watch-it flag, not an order.

Bottom line: this page is a weather report — how much wind and when, never which direction. Entry decisions stay with your actual signals (VIX rules, GEX, the morning message). Use this to decide if you're paying list price or clearance for movement, and what hours to brace for.
